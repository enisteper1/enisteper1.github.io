Basic BM25, optimized BM25 and vector search

November 2026 · 5 min read

How much does retrieval improve when we tune BM25, and how does it compare with vector search? This experiment compares three configurations on Quora, WANDS and HotpotQA: basic BM25, BM25 with optimized field weights and parameters, and pretrained GTE-small.

There is no single winner across datasets and metrics. GTE-small has a large lead on Quora. Optimized BM25 is close on HotpotQA and slightly ahead on MRR and recall. On WANDS, optimized BM25 has higher nDCG and recall, although differences in field inputs and evaluation qualify that result.

What are we comparing?

BM25 scores matching words; field weights decide which parts of a document count more, while k1 and b control repetition and length normalization. GTE-small encodes a query and document into vectors and ranks by cosine similarity. That gives it a way to match related wording beyond the lexical matches BM25 relies on.

The vector run uses pretrained GTE-small, one normalized 384-dimensional vector per document, and exact search over the full corpus. It is not fine-tuned for these datasets. Both BM25 configurations use regex plus Porter stemming. Basic BM25 concatenates its input fields and uses k1=1.2, b=0.75. Optimized BM25 uses separate weighted fields where available, then tunes k1 and b. The field-weighting and parameter-tuning articles explain those choices:

Quora and HotpotQA use their full test sets: 10,000 and 7,405 queries. WANDS uses 479 judged queries. The corpus sizes are 522,931 questions, 5,233,329 passages and 42,994 products, respectively. Metrics are consistent within each dataset; their different relevance definitions make absolute scores across datasets less useful.

These are comparisons of existing runs, not a new joint experiment. For Quora and HotpotQA, both pipelines use the full BEIR test splits and exclude query/document self-matches. Their package versions differ. No paired confidence intervals are reported for GTE versus optimized BM25, so small differences should not be treated as established improvements.

Quora: tuning does not close the gap

GTE-small reaches 0.8802 nDCG, versus 0.8011 for tuned BM25: a lead of about 7.91 points. Parameter tuning barely moves the lexical baseline. GTE also leads on MRR and Recall@100.

A plausible reason is the task itself: two questions can ask the same thing using different words. Field weights cannot help a single-field corpus, and changing saturation or length penalties cannot create missing lexical matches. An embedding model can represent similarities that survive a change in wording. The size of the gap is consistent with that explanation, though the aggregate results do not prove which query types account for it.

HotpotQA: optimized BM25 is close

GTE’s nDCG lead over basic BM25 is 4.28 points. Against weighted, parameter-tuned BM25, it is only 0.55 points: 0.6378 versus 0.6323. Optimizing the lexical system closes roughly 87% of the original gap.

The remaining difference also depends on the metric. Tuned BM25 reaches 0.8049 MRR, slightly above GTE’s 0.8008. Recall@100 is 0.7772 versus 0.7758. GTE has higher nDCG and Recall@10 (0.6580 versus 0.6521), so neither method leads everywhere.

The parameter sweep shows that lowering k1 mattered much more than field weighting alone. That suggests much of the original gap reflected how BM25 scored its matches, rather than a limitation that only embeddings could address. It does not establish that the methods retrieve the same passages, or that the small remaining differences are reliable.

WANDS: the numbers favor BM25, with a qualification

Tuned BM25 reaches 0.7269 nDCG against GTE’s 0.7174, about 0.95 points higher. Its Recall@100 is also higher: 0.4172 versus 0.3801. GTE retains the higher MRR, 0.7147 versus 0.7033: it places the first Exact product earlier on average, while BM25 does better on the graded top-10 ranking and broader candidate coverage.

There are two reasons not to call this a clean win for lexical retrieval. Tuned BM25 uses five product fields; GTE sees only name, category and description. Also, BM25’s fixed field weights were selected using these queries during field-weight selection. Its cross-validation holds out queries for parameter selection only, so the result is conditional on that earlier choice.

Beating unweighted concatenation does not establish that vector search outperforms a carefully configured product-search system. Giving GTE the same five fields and comparing both systems on untouched queries would make the next comparison more decisive.

If the next stage reranks 100 candidates, coverage matters: a reranker cannot recover a relevant document that never arrived. If the first result matters most, MRR may tell a different story. That is why “does vector search win?” needs a metric as well as a dataset.

There is also an operational difference: dense retrieval needs document encoding, vector storage and query inference. These saved runs are not a controlled latency benchmark, so their timings do not establish a production speed advantage. Query-level outcomes help assess whether combining the methods is worth testing; no hybrid result is measured here.

Do they win on the same queries?

The advantage changes sides on all three datasets. Matching queries by ID, GTE-small has higher nDCG@10 on 2,840 Quora queries, but optimized BM25 wins on 987. On WANDS, BM25 wins more often, yet GTE is better on 155 of the 479 queries. Neither method dominates query by query.

HotpotQA makes the complementarity clearest. The average nDCG scores are close, but BM25 is better on 2,008 queries and GTE on 2,245. Together, those are 57.4% of the test queries where one method scores higher than the other. Similar averages hide substantial differences in which queries each system handles well.

To measure the room for query-level selection, imagine an oracle that knows the relevance labels and always picks the better of the two complete rankings. Its mean nDCG would be:

These numbers show complementary ranking strengths and give a reason to test query routing or hybrid retrieval. They are not gains a deployed system can claim: the oracle uses answers unavailable at search time, and it selects one whole ranking rather than merging results. Per-query scores also do not measure overlap between relevant documents; candidate complementarity requires a separate comparison of retrieved document IDs.

The takeaway

There is no single winner across datasets, metrics and queries. GTE-small leads clearly on Quora, while optimized BM25 is competitive on HotpotQA and offers stronger coverage on WANDS in this comparison. Better average ranking does not mean better results for every query.

The two methods have complementary strengths: each handles queries the other handles less well. That makes combining them worth testing, rather than assuming one should replace the other. The right choice depends on the balance of ranking quality, candidate coverage and computational cost—and any hybrid gain still needs to be measured.
